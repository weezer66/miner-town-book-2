# Chapter 2, round 2: Opus and Fable drafts, combined, blind-compared (2026-10-20)
**The review:** 6.5–7.5 from the models. The middle third worked like a press conference, with three deferrals ("I do not know," "the rest of it is long," "Tomorrow. All of it").

**The fixes (in both drafts):**
- **The birthright exchange is cut.** The opening plants the papers instead: *"Wrong question. Ask what was taken from your parents, and who signed for it."*
- **"Absconded" is now a deadline for deciding, and a protection:** *"It is the kindest word it has. Nobody bills a street for a boy who ran… Before then, you choose. Stay, or go home with a story I give you, one that holds."*
- **Flitzberg's fortune-cookie lines are cut:** "lied to with confidence," "no reason yet to value," and "A dead man answers nothing," which becomes *"That is why I came in alone."*
- **The ten stones get a gloss:** *"so the rest of them could drop through, and had stayed behind to do it."*
- **The promise recap is trimmed.**
- *"She'd never been lifted. In Miner Town you got dragged."*

**How the merge was built:**
- **Opus's draft is the base.** It keeps Camilla's ration card, which Ch19's clerk depends on ("Last drawn the same morning as the Parker boy's"). It also puts Ben's "no counter" line as his answer to Gretchen's four-day wait: *"So she stands there four days, and then they write it down, and she stands there anyway."*
- **From Fable:** "one that holds," which sets up Ch6's *"a story that holds."*

- *"Miner Town does not go looking for children who walk away"* (Haiku called it the strongest deadline line). Its old second half, "It goes looking for the ones who come back," is left out; Sonnet flagged it as a logic slip.
