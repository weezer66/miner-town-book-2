## Required changes
1. **Cut the birthright exchange,** from Gage's *"And this birthright," he said. "Say what it means or I'm walking."* through Ben's *"There's no counter in Miner Town where you line up and ask for your freedom back"* and Flitzberg's *"Which is why the rest of it is long."*
   - Chapter 6's wall of deeds and signatures delivers "there are papers" much better, and Ch2 only spoils it.
   - **Ben's "There's no counter in Miner Town…" line was a favorite with readers.** Re-home it somewhere it still makes sense, for example in reaction to the absconded speech or to Flitzberg's refusal to tell Gretchen. Cut it only if it truly has no home.
   - **The bridge to "Gage opened his mouth. / 'Gage.' Camilla got her good elbow under herself…" must still flow.**
2. **Plant the papers early instead.** Flitzberg's opening line is *"The question is not what I did to you. … The question is: what will you do to regain the freedom that was once your birthright?"* Replace it with something close to: *"Wrong question. Ask what was taken from your parents, and who signed for it."*
   - Ben's *"What does that mean?"* and *"You are ready now. Ready for the journey ahead."* may need adjusting so the exchange still makes sense. Keep the overheard-while-waking feel.
3. **Turn "Absconded" from a stall into a clock.** Three test readers misread it three different ways.
   - Make the four days **a deadline for deciding**, and make *Absconded* **the protection**.
   - The editor's model: *"In four days, the mine writes one word next to your three names. Absconded. It is the kindest word it has: nobody bills a street for a boy who ran. Before then, you choose. Stay, or go home with a story I give you. Walk out tonight, bleeding, with no story…"*
   - Keep the consequences of walking out tonight: rations short, quotas up, a neighbor's door kicked in. Keep Camilla's ration card stopping too, but fold it in so it doesn't muddy the logic. If the boys are written off as absconded, the household stops drawing for them; you decide whether Camilla's card still belongs.
   - **This sets up Chapter 6,** where Flitzberg says *"Then you go home, with a story that holds."*
   - **"Absconded" must stay as the word.** Chapter 19 echoes it: *"Absconded. Slots released."*
   - "Tomorrow. All of it" should then read as a deal, not a stall.
4. **Cut Flitzberg's fortune-cookie lines:**
   - *"You have been lied to with confidence your whole lives."* End that speech on *"I will not pretend to more than that."*
   - *"which you have no reason yet to value"*. End on *"You have my word."* or similar.
   - *"A dead man answers nothing."* Rework or cut it, but keep the sense that he chose to be within reach of Gage's hand.
5. **Gloss "the boy who'd held ten stones open over a lake of fire."** Readers couldn't place it. In Book 1's last trial room, Otto held a ring of ten floating stones open over a lava floor so the others could drop through, and he stayed behind.
   - Later chapters reuse "held ten stones open over the fire," so **keep the image recognizable.** Add a clause so a reader who doesn't remember Book 1 gets it, e.g. *"…so the rest of them could drop through."*
6. **Trim the promise recap.** The current text is: *"He'd promised her, outside the four doors: if I tell you to stop, you stop. He'd kept it once already, with a shock still humming in his bones."* The epigraph already quotes the promise, so this is a third telling. Use something like *"He'd promised her that. He'd kept it once already, with the shock still in his bones."* **Keep the finger-by-finger release** that follows.
7. **The lift.** The current text is *"Nobody in Miner Town had ever lifted a miner's body like that."* The editor prefers *"She'd never been lifted. In Miner Town you got dragged."*
