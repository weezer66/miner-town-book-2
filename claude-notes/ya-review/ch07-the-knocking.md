# Ch7 The Knocking: YA review

**POV:** Elara · **Length:** ~2,550 words · **Dialogue:** ~2% · **Job:** aftermath; the summons; the Corridor of Knowledge; the committee; the raised palm

## What already works for YA
- **Exhaustion done physically:** falling asleep on the table, drool on her cheek, the dust crescent on her sleeve. Very real, very teen.
- **The 5 a.m. knock**, three times. That's good dread.
- **The raised palm that stops her breath** is a strong, eerie closing hook.

## Gaps
1. **The first 900 words are her rereading the same packet** while dozing. That's realistic, but it repeats itself ("read the same line twice", "a second time, then a third", "read one more sentence"). We get the point quickly.
2. **The karma meditation** ("What she had done would have to be paid for… Time would decide…") is reflection with no action.
3. **The committee members are introduced by costume and aura** (robe, stones, cards, shawl), all in paragraph-long portraits in a row. It's a mood piece, but four portraits back-to-back stall the scene.
4. **Elara does nothing** between the knock and the palm except dress and walk.

## Suggested changes
- **High: halve the packet-reading section.** Keep the 02:13 auto-generation detail and the drool image; cut the second and third rereads.
- **High: give her one small act on the walk.** For example, she stops at the glass case with the brass measuring arm, the one she used to draw as a student. Her fingers want a pencil, and that's what steadies her. It's active, it's hers, and it seeds the artist thread from Ch3.
- **Medium: tighten the four portraits** to about 60% each. Let Draṣṭā's cards and Sarvajña's stones carry them. The Ancient One's portrait can stay full, since she's the payoff.
- **Medium: the guards.** One of them is young, younger than her, and won't meet her eyes. One line, and it humanizes the system's arm.
- **Low: the gurukul rooms** (river model, painting, stringed instrument) are lovely. Cut one of the three.

## Keep
"What did you do with what you knew?" is the chapter's question, asked by the objects. Keep it exactly.
