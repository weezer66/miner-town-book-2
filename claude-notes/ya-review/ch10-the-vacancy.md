# Ch10 The Vacancy: YA review

**POV:** Elara · **Length:** ~3,150 words · **Dialogue:** ~38% · **Job:** #4055 choice; the doubled transfer; OG found

## What already works for YA
- **The cafeteria as cover** (trays crashing, noise breaking speech into harmless pieces) is a great, ordinary, teen-familiar setting for a conspiracy.
- **Elara choosing the #4055 timing,** with boundaries ("no one touches the boys' choices after this"), is agency with ethics.
- **"OG" at the bottom of a document** is a clean, eerie reveal.

## Gaps
1. **Kavik's inspection-timing explanation runs about 230 words in a single speech.** It's the mechanism of a key plot device, but it's delivered as a lecture in a noisy cafeteria where they should be talking low and fast.
2. **Anandi and Kavik are warm but similar.** Both are wise and both are measured. A YA mentor pair works better with contrast (one blunt, one gentle).
3. **The archive scene** (public copy → departmental → fiscal → native → legacy firewall → Sākṣī) has a lot of layers for one reveal.
4. **⚠ Continuity:** Flitzberg later asks "whose hand?" about #4055 (Ch18, Ch21), and this chapter has already shown the reader that it was Elara's. Decide how the climax handles that (see continuity sheet §6.2).

## Suggested changes
- **High: break Kavik's explanation into back-and-forth.** Let Elara interrupt with the obvious objection ("Then any clerk could…") and Kavik answer short. Three exchanges instead of one block.
- **Medium: differentiate the mentors.** Make Kavik dry and a little funny (he's already complaining about the thinned stew; extend that), and make Anandi the one who presses. A YA reader should be able to tell who's speaking without the tags.
- **Medium: collapse the archive layers.** Name two (the public copy, the native record) and the firewall. The middle layers can be one sentence.
- **Low: "She had chosen one small condition in a future she could not see."** This summarizes the scene. Consider ending the cafeteria section on her folding the notice instead.
- **Low: "Sarvajñā" vs "Sarvajña"** spelling varies in this chapter (and in Ch8 and Ch11). Pick one.

## Keep
"If the city insists on giving him a dead man's position, it can give him his own dead." That's one of the best lines in the book.
