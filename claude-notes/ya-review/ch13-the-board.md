# Ch13 The Board: YA review

**POV:** Ravi, then Daren · **Length:** ~5,500 words · **Dialogue:** ~4% · **Job:** the chess match; the interruption; the black king; Daren in the closet; Meru

## What already works for YA
- **A living chess board with elephants, camels and a queen on a mare,** played before thousands. It's a set piece with real spectacle. YA readers will love it (think Hogwarts' giant chess).
- **"We stop here."** The interruption is a great twist.
- **The black king on his desk with "YOU ARE WELCOME,"** then the reveal that Daren was hiding in the storage recess, watching him sleep. This is genuinely creepy, and it's the best chapter-ending reversal in Act Two.

## Gaps
1. **⚠ Ravi's age.** This is the chapter where he's most clearly a *young* man ("the young apprentice", "the young representative"). But Ch17, 19 and 24 give him eleven years of service (~28 years old). For YA, keep him young. See `00-index.md`.
2. **The morning routine runs ~1,200 words** before the match: quarters, mirrors, cuff, corridor, gymnasium, twenty laps.
3. **Move-by-move announcer calls** are fun for a few moves, and then a chess-literate reader starts checking them and a non-chess reader starts skimming.
4. **Ravi barely speaks.** Two lines across 5,500 words. The match is "chess as dialogue" (per the decision log), but Ravi's inner voice is also mostly analysis.
5. **The Meru coda at the end** (stewards, terraces, attendants) is good worldbuilding, but it comes after the chapter's best beat and deflates it.

## Suggested changes
- **High: make Ravi's age clear here.** He's the youngest in the top ten, and the elders' notes say so. That makes the lottery win, and later "the lottery was told to fall to you" (Ch17), sting harder.
- **High: compress the morning to ~500 words.** Keep:
  - the two mirrors ("the one he was about to show");
  - the cuff script;
  - laps 15–20 with the board in his head.

  Cut the shelving and record-issuance explanation.
- **Medium: cut the announcer calls by half.** Keep the camel's dangerous move, the elephant's file, the queen's revelation, and the check. Let the crowd's reaction carry the middle.
- **Medium: give Ravi one feeling-line during the match** that isn't analysis. For example, when the queen moves: *"He had been so sure. He was always so sure."* YA readers need the person, not only the player.
- **Medium: move the Meru coda** to Ch17's opening, or cut it to its last paragraph. End Ch13 on Daren smiling at the window, or on the corridor where everyone looks away.
- **Low: the Hall of Arguments and the Corridor of Unsaid Things** are both wonderful. Keep one in full and reduce the other to a single image.

## Keep
"Not true. / Not false. / Useful." That's the house's whole philosophy in three lines.
