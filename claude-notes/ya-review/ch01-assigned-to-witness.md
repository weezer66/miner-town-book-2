# Ch1 Assigned to Witness: YA review

**POV:** Elara · **Length:** ~2,400 words · **Dialogue:** ~12% · **Job:** hook, world, the four monitors, UNASSIGNED ×3

## What already works for YA
- The **blood test** is an excellent cold open for teen readers. It's a body being judged by a machine, and "Empathy surge risk: low" is a line a 15-year-old will screenshot.
- **Four houses, four uniforms, four stations** reads like a sorting ceremony. YA readers love this shape (houses, factions, districts). Lean into it.
- **"Assign Gage to me."** A clear moment of choice and claiming. This is her first act of agency.
- Otto "reading him like a source" closes the chapter on a strong hook.

## Gaps
1. **Nobody's age is stated except Elara's** (19, and only in Ch3). For a YA shelf, the reader needs to know on page one that **these four are barely older than the kids they're watching**. Ravi is a particular problem: Ch17, 19 and 24 give him "eleven years of service," which makes him ~28. See the cross-book note in `00-index.md`.
2. **The world paragraph** (Trinity-1/2/3 and Meru) arrives as a block before we've met anyone. A teen reader skims it.
3. **Elara has no texture yet.** She's all observation and ethics. There's no habit, no joke, no private object.
4. **The other three are introduced by doctrine** (Chanakya calm, Kubera confidence, Ashoka force). Each needs one human detail the reader can hold.

5. **⚠ Book 1 check: Gage's weight.** Ch1 says "Two hundred eighty pounds." Book 1 says **220** three times ("his 220-pound frame", "a 220-pound antenna", "220 pounds of fever and bone"). Elara's whole deduction ("someone has been feeding him off the books") still works at 220 on a starch ration; just change the number.
6. **Book 1 check: Elara's routine.** Book 1's cutaways have Elara start work at **04:00** under lavender light, held at 65 bpm, and call her a "Manager." Ch1 wakes her at 3:00 a.m. That's compatible (she wakes at 3 and starts at 4), and a nod to the 65 bpm would reward Book 1 readers.

## Suggested changes
- **High: "Two hundred eighty pounds" → "Two hundred twenty pounds."**
- **High: state the cohort's youth.** One sentence, ideally Elara's thought as the doors open: *"Four of them, and not one older than twenty. Trinity staffed the Initiation with new graduates on purpose: people who hadn't yet learned to flinch."* (This is the rewrite plan's §1 idea, in-world.)
- **High: give each monitor one non-doctrinal detail on entry.** For example:
  - Ravi: ink on his cuff, or he counts the doors.
  - Daren: a cufflink he straightens twice.
  - Rohan: a split knuckle, healing, from the dojo kids (this pays off in Ch15).
- **Medium: break the world paragraph in two.** Put Trinity-1/2/3 after the Skylark ride, as she looks down. Keep the Meru "merely" line where it is; it's the best line in the paragraph.
- **Medium: give Elara one private object or habit.** Ch3 gives her red paint and sunsets. Seed it here: a pencil callus, or she sketches the console layout in a margin out of habit. YA readers bond through specifics.
- **Low: her catching Ravi's wrist** at the High Command button is the chapter's only touch. Let it cost her a beat. She notices she did it, and he notices her noticing.

## Keep
The epigraph pairing (the Initiation as culling ↔ assignment day) is strong. Daren's "Three rounding errors the system was too honest to hide" is good villain voice.
