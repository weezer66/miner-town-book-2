# Ch15 The Guardians: YA review

**POV:** Rohan · **Length:** ~4,850 words · **Dialogue:** ~2% · **Job:** Rohan's two days of doubt; the Little Dragons; the eighth-dan ceremony; Master Vayun

## What already works for YA
- **The Little Dragons class** is a gift. Claudia's "That falling is not the end of the lesson" and Rohan as "a beloved bear who happened to know how to stop a fight" make the most lovable version of the book's toughest character. YA readers will adore him from here on.
- **Guards vs. guardians** is a clear, teen-legible theme.
- **Master Vayun's white-again belt** is a lovely detail with real meaning.
- **"He used your name."** A great small hook.

## Gaps
1. **The first ~1,800 words are Rohan replaying footage and reasoning about authority vs. justice,** circling the same question ("How could preserving that be wrong?") several times. This is the decision log's 13–15 low-dialogue run, and it's the most at-risk stretch in the book for a YA reader.
2. **The maxims list** is good, but it's followed by more abstract reasoning rather than a scene.
3. **The dojo should come first.** It's the hook, and it's buried in the middle.
4. **Japanese belt text** (二段, ローハン) is still on the language-check tracker.

## Suggested changes
- **High: restructure the order:**
  1. **Open in the dojo** with Claudia and Dev.
  2. Let Rohan's doubt surface *through* the lesson: Claudia's fall, "responsible for what your strength does next."
  3. Then a shorter reflection (cut ~60%), with the footage replays intercut in two short flashes.
  4. Then the summons and the ceremony.

  This turns the abstract argument into something a reader watches happen.
- **High: cut the reflection** to its best turns:
  - "The words fair chance troubled him more than the word override";
  - "authority over everyone was a debt owed to everyone";
  - "You stood where Ashoka should have been standing."
- **Medium: give one Little Dragon a line to Rohan** that cuts him. For example, Tavi asks, *"Do you ever lose?"* and Rohan can't answer, because he's been losing an argument with himself for two days.
- **Low: the ministers and generals** are listed three times. Once is enough.

## Keep
The final beat ("the ceremony had something waiting for him") is a good chapter-ender. Keep the goosebumps line.
