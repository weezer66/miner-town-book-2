# Ch19 The Journey Ahead: YA review

**POV:** Flitzberg → Ravi → Rohan · **Length:** ~4,700 words · **Dialogue:** ~33% · **Paragraphs over 150 words:** 7 · **Job:** picks up Book 1's last line; truths owed; Ravi's slip; the Dhammapala's warning

## What already works for YA
- **Starting on Book 1's cliffhanger,** the boy's hand on Flitzberg's throat, is exactly right. Returning readers will cheer.
- **Flitzberg's "I do not know"** about Otto, and his refusal to lie ("You have been lied to with confidence your whole lives"), is a great way to earn teen readers' trust in an adult.
- **The medic turning her case toward Ben** is a tiny, perfect act of respect.
- **Ben: "There's no counter in Miner Town where you can stand in line and ask for your freedom back."** That's the teen cast's best line in Book 2.

## Gaps
1. **⚠ Book 1 check: who lowered the stones.** "Flitzberg looked at the small boy who had lowered ten stones into a lake of fire": in context he's looking at Otto, but Ch18 and line 83 here call *Ben* "the small one." It's ambiguous. In Book 1, Otto lifts and lowers the ten stones; Ben drops the compass.
2. **⚠ Book 1 check: Flitzberg's look.** He's "the life of one old man." Book 1 gives him "smooth pale skin, full cheeks." See the Ch14 note.
3. **The opening is ~700 words of Flitzberg's reasoning** about why he walked in alone, *while a boy is choking him.* It's the right reasoning at the wrong moment. It stops the most physical beat in the chapter.
4. **The teens get the stage and then mostly listen.** Gage asks two questions, Ben one, and Camilla is offstage on a stretcher.
5. **Book 1's ending had four faces and "three looking back."** The emotional fact of that scene is Otto gone, and Gage and Ben's grief is subdued here.
6. **The Ravi section is ~1,800 words of interior.** See the Ch24 note on Ravi's columns.

## Suggested changes
- **High: move Flitzberg's reasoning after the grip opens.** Open on the hand, the trembling, and "You are angry at the right person." Then, once Gage steps back, Flitzberg can think (briefly) about why he came in alone. Cut it by half.
- **High: give the boys more voice and more mess.** Ben is crying and not hiding it (good). Let Gage say something raw and teen-sharp, e.g. *"You don't get to say 'chosen.' You don't get to say anything nice."* Book 1's Gage "rasps" and swears by implication; here he's polite.
- **High: contractions for the teens.** Book 1's dialogue uses contractions about 6× as often as Book 2's. Gage's "What did we ever do to you?" is from Book 1, and his lines around it should match that register (*"That's not good enough"* already does).
- **Medium: clarify the stones line:** *"Flitzberg looked past Ben to Otto, the boy who had held ten stones over a lake of fire…"*
- **Medium: trim Ravi's memo-mechanics paragraph** (petition office, subject tags, supporting exhibit). Keep "The city preferred its stories to have authors" and the slip of paper.
- **Low: the Dhammapala scene** with the oil lamp is quiet and good. Keep it as is.
