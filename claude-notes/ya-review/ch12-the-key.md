# Ch12 The Key: YA review

**POV:** Elara · **Length:** ~3,350 words · **Dialogue:** ~21% · **Job:** maglev to Ashoka; the confession; Rohan's pledge; the handshake

> Decision log: the confession is spoken ("Before the boys reached the gates, I altered the record"), and the ground-cast scene was cut. Both kept.

## What already works for YA
- **The infant pointing at Elara's collar** is the warmest moment in the book so far. It shows Elara laughing.
- **Ashoka HQ** (names cut into the walls, the recruit who stops because "the threat had ended") is worldbuilding through action.
- **The confession**, and Rohan's reply: "Ashoka does not retract its word because the truth arrives with details." This is the emotional high point of the Elara–Rohan thread.

## Gaps
1. **The maglev science paragraph** (superconducting coils, traveling wave) reads like a textbook. One sentence of it is plenty for a YA reader.
2. **About 900 words of travel and architecture** before she reaches Rohan.
3. **The four-gana crisis speech** ("Ashoka will spend its strength on containment… Drona will turn inward…") is Elara explaining the plot's theory to Rohan in one block.
4. **Rohan's "blue eyes" are introduced here.** It's the first time the book lets Elara notice his appearance, and then nothing is done with it.
5. **The closing paragraph's "two were better than one"** is a little flat for the end of a big chapter.

## Suggested changes
- **High: cut the maglev science to one sentence.** For example: *"The train rode a hand's width above the rail on nothing at all, which Drona's instructors liked to call the city's best joke about itself."*
- **High: split Elara's crisis speech** with one challenge from Rohan, so it becomes an argument. *"You're describing every house doing its job." "Yes. That's the trap."*
- **Medium: the blue-eyes moment.** If you want a slow-burn thread (which YA readers will look for between these two whether you plant it or not), this is the place: one beat where his direct look makes her lose her sentence, and she resents it. If you *don't* want romance, cut the eye color and keep "his directness," so readers aren't primed for something that never comes.
- **Medium: end on the handshake.** Cut "Drona was reaching toward Ashoka…" and the last paragraph, or reduce them to one line about the doubled transfer becoming "shelves and scaffolding" (that image is good).
- **Low: the passengers.** The Kubera allocation officer, the Chanakya rep with text in her jacket, and the Ashoka responder are a nice trio. Keep two.

## Keep
"Elara: 'No. I am not certain. If I waited for certainty, it would arrive in the form of the damage itself.'" That's a great YA heroine line.
