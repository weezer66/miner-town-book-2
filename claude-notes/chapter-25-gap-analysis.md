## B. Logic gaps

**B1. Why does Ravi take the report at all? (High)**
The revision says the report "told him nothing new, and nothing of what the inquiry had decided." Then he takes a sealed case's original overnight, leaves a paper trail with his signature, and promises it back "tomorrow" (which "was not a promise he knew he could keep"). If it holds nothing new, the risk has no reason, and the "not a promise he could keep" line suggests a plan the scene never hints at.
- **Fix:** give him one reason the reader can feel without revealing contents (Plan C). Either:
  - **a.** He wants the one copy of the case that no hand can rewrite, because after Ch17 he trusts nothing on a screen, *including his own memory of what he reported*. Say it in one line.
  - **b.** He means to put it somewhere: the cloakroom pocket, under the second slip, as an answer to whoever wrote "Room Four. Two hours." That ties the scene to Ch24 and starts the convergence (via Kaan) without revealing anything.

**B2. A dark terminal in a watched building (Medium)**
Ravi "left the terminal dark in his pocket" inside the archive. By the book's own rule (Ch24, Rohan), a body without a live terminal makes every sensor ask "who are you" and get no answer, and "someone will be sent to find out why." Either the fiber archive has no sensors (state it: paper vaults kept instrument-free on purpose, like the dead room), or his dark terminal is itself an anomaly someone will notice. It could be a good one.

**B3. The Ancient One endangers her own witness (High)**
She tells the Master there is a witness. Per the tracker, the Master is the one person who knows exactly what the band is and who wears it. He only has to ask Daren where he was last night, and two bare-wristed people in his corridor becomes a short list. The chapter treats the call as a step forward. It's actually the most dangerous thing anyone has done to Elara in the book, and nobody notices. Either make her notice (one line from Elara: "You told him there was a witness." / "I told him there was one. I did not tell him where to look."), or don't make the call.

**B4. Rohan hesitating over the Dhammapala contradicts Ch19 (High)**
Lines 185–189 have Rohan weighing "the risk of telling his Grandmaster." But **Ch19** settles this: "the charge had come from her, so the unit was Ashoka's — and a secret unit that answered to no Grandmaster would make him the very thing he was hunting." He reports everything to her except his people's faces. He wouldn't hesitate over a suspect's name. The only thing he *can't* pass on is what he doesn't know (Patal Loka, which Elara now knows and he doesn't).
- **Fix:** flip the beat. He *will* tell the Dhammapala, and the tension is Elara realizing she now holds a secret *he* can't have, which breaks their Ch18 rule ("This is not your case with my help. It is ours."). That's a much better YA ending beat (see C6).

**B5. How does the Ancient One know they were in the corridor? (Medium)**
"You saw the hand return" (line 99). The plan was made in the dead room, which nobody can overhear. The Sākṣī would show the hand's orders at that hour, but not who was watching. Give her one line: "Anandi told me where you meant to stand. The wall told me who walked past you."

**B6. Contradictory instruction (Low)**
Line 111 says "do not contact the Master of the Reserve… If he learns that we have named him…". "Him" reads as the Master himself, and then she calls the Master herself thirty lines later. Elara would never contact him anyway. Change to: "If the hand learns we have named it before we know what it did, the next move will be its."

**B7. Unaddressed fallout from Ch24 (Medium)**
The chapter is the morning after the corridor, and it skips three consequences the previous chapters set up:
- **The sensors that got no answer** (Ch24: "Someone will be sent to find out why").
- **Daren after the Master's warning** ("your hand is wrong").
- **Baju's notebook** in Daren's coat, which maps the corridor Elara and Rohan just stood in.

At least one should surface. The cleanest is a short Daren section reading the tower digest (beat sheet §C3).

**B8. Who decides Otto is "ready"? (Low)**
The medic decides alone. Apart from A1, a Patal Loka medic would at least alert Flitzberg before draining the office's most valuable candidate. One line: "She had already sent word upstairs."

## C. YA gaps and misses

**C1. The reunion misses Book 1's biggest open wound (High)**
Book 1's last exchange between Gage and Otto: Gage says "Promise me you'll jump through after me," and Otto answers "I promise, brother." Then Otto lets the stones close over him. **This is the promise Ch25 should pay off**, and it isn't mentioned. Suggested:

> "You promised," Gage said. It came out wrong, rough, nothing like what he meant.
>
> Otto looked at him for a long moment. "I jumped." His voice was barely there. "Took me a while."

These two lines will land harder with readers than anything else in the chapter.

**C2. Four teenagers, no mess (High)**
Nobody laughs, cries, touches him, or says a teenage thing. Ben's "But we're all here" (new) is good, so build on it. Give the scene one touch (Gage's hand on the back of Otto's neck), one tear (Ben, unhidden, as in Ch19), and one joke (Camilla about the arm: "Don't stare. You've looked worse."). Book 1's crew is warm and rough with each other ("That's how." / the slap). Ch25's is polite.

**C3. Otto barely speaks (Medium)**
He says two words in his own POV section. After a whole chapter (Ch23) inside his head, the reader wants his voice back. Add "Did they hold?" (the stones), or have him notice Ben's empty pocket ("Where's your dad's compass?"). That second line pays off Book 1's sacrifice of the compass. Ben: "Lava."

**C4. The epigraph doesn't fit the chapter (Medium)**
"The Engine is blind without the Eyes" is about Gage trusting Camilla's lead (Book 1 Ch22). Ch25 is about Otto coming back and Elara naming Daren, and the Gage/Camilla dynamic isn't in it. Better-fitting verbatim options:
- Book 1 Ch25: *"I promise, brother."* Short, and it would set up C1. It is verbatim; check the attribution format.
- Book 1 Ch7: *"the drone recognizes you locally and stores the package, but the upload to Jung only happens when the inspection completes."* This fits Elara's stored face (beat sheet).

**C5. Elara receives instead of acts (High)**
Her section runs dream, then summons, then briefing, then an order to "stay in the shadows," then she reports to Rohan. Every important thing is done *to* her or *for* her, and the YA protagonist in Act Three should be driving. Give her one active move before the summons: she checks the tower's guest register and finds the door assigned to Daren for the Sector 1 cycle and never released (beat sheet §C2). Then *she* brings the Ancient One a paper fact. The Ancient One's "Do not turn recognition into proof" becomes "You've brought me a room. Now don't let it become a verdict." She's earned her place at the table.

**C6. The ending is a non-decision (Medium)**
The last beat is Rohan not deciding something he has already decided (B4). A YA chapter should end on a turn. Options:
- **a.** The secret between them: she knows the name on the map and he doesn't. "Is there something you're not telling me?" "Yes." They've never had a secret from each other. End there.
- **b.** The sensors: Nayan's runner brings the tower bulletin, and two unidentified persons on the fourth-level stair are being sought. The hunters are being hunted.
- **c.** A Daren last line: he opens Baju's notebook to the circled door, and it's his.

**C7. Ravi's scene has no heartbeat (Medium)**
It's a procedure: index, desk, register, sleeve, ledger, gate. One physical tell would make it his: a thumb on his old handwriting, his pulse when the clerk says "sealed above your grade," or a glance at the porter who brought the box up (the Kaan seed from the beat sheet).

**C8. No ticking clock (Low)**
After Ch24's council vote and the Master's warning, the chapter loses urgency. The meeting "before you make your next move" has no time on it. Give it one: "Tonight," or "Before the next council."

**C9. The chapter is short and missing a POV (Low)**
At 2,197 words it's the shortest chapter since Ch2, and it's the only recent chapter without Daren, even though the previous chapter ended on his strongest scene. Adding C1–C3 (~250 words) and a Daren section (~1,000) gets it to about 3,500, in line with Ch24.
