# Chapter 18: In the Blue (II)

*"Promise me," Gage said. His voice broke. "Promise me you'll jump through after me." They both knew it was a lie. The circle was barely shoulder width and shrinking. But Otto looked at his best friend and gave him the only gift he had left — a reason to move. "I promise, brother."*

— Book 1, Chapter 25: Don't Do It

## Now: The tank

The warm went out and the cool came in, and the voice came down with it.

Otto knew the voice now, in the sense that he knew the shape of a chair in a dark room he'd slept in a long while. It talked and stopped. It left its gap. It went on. He'd stopped waiting for it to want anything from him. It talked like rain on a roof, and he lay under it.

Then a word came down the shaft nearly whole.

Most of them arrived as gravel, worn round, no edges to catch on. This one had edges. It had a hard sound at the front and a hiss in the middle, and it landed in the blue and didn't sink straight away.

*Gretchen.*

He knew it. Knowing it was like finding a step in the dark with your heel: a jolt, and then the whole stair there behind it. A girl. Small, in a coat too big for her, at a fence, with her hands through the wire up to the wrist. The voice's sister. The voice had a sister, so the voice had a name, and the name was coming up to him out of the deep place like a bubble, slow, and he reached for it—

Something in him reached.

It was small. He couldn't have said what part of him did it, or where. It felt like one stone in a ring of ten shifting in its socket, a grain of a turn, the heavy one wanting to move again. A lift. No more than a lift. Nothing he'd have called a push, if anyone had asked, which no one did.

Then the stillness took it back.

It did that without hurry, as it did everything. The stone settled. The deep floor came up under him and was level again, and the blue was the blue, and he hadn't gone anywhere.

But the voice had stopped.

It stopped in the middle, on the climb of the sentence, where nobody stops on purpose. The gap came too early. It ran too long. It was the wrong shape for a gap, and he knew the gaps, he'd lain under hundreds of them. This one had a hole in it.

Then the voice breathed in. He heard that. The breath came down the shaft clearer than any word had.

And it began the sentence again, from the start.

*Gretchen.*

The same word, the same edges. Then the gap. The same gap, laid down to the beat, as if the voice had measured it against something.

He understood it then. He didn't get the words back; they'd gone to gravel again. He understood what the gap was *for.* It was a hole left in the air the exact size of an answer. It had always been that. The voice came down here and left a hole for him, and came again and left another, and he had lain beneath them like a man asleep under a dripping tap, and all this while it had been waiting on him.

He couldn't fill it. He tried, and the trying went into the stillness with the rest. The gap ran its length and closed, and the voice went on.

*Fine*, said the part of him that still talked back. *Leave another one. I'm not going anywhere.*

The voice left another one.

---

He thought about the promise after that. He hadn't meant to. It came up like a bruise comes, the second day.

*Promise me you'll jump through after me.*

*I promise, brother.*

It had sat on him since the stones. He knew its weight to the ounce. Two pieces of a thing he'd said so a big fool would climb, and the big fool had climbed, and the pieces had come down into the blue with him and lain across his chest, and every time he'd gathered himself to rise they had been there first.

Only now he'd reached. He'd felt a stone move. And there was a question in that, and the question was: broken by whom?

He hadn't jumped. True. He'd said he would, and the circle had shut, and he'd stayed. But a promise didn't finish when the hole closed. A promise finished when the one who'd made it quit. Nobody had asked him if he'd quit. He hadn't been in a fit state to be asked.

*I promise, brother.* He turned it over. It didn't weigh what it had weighed. He checked again, the way you check a tooth you were sure was loose. It wasn't lying on him at all.

It was hanging.

A rope, let down a shaft. He could feel the length of it going up through the blue, away past the warm and the cool, past the voice's gap, into a dark he couldn't see the top of. And at the top, where it was tied off, a face. Upturned at the hole. The scar-map white across the brow. Wearing the look it wore when it had made up its mind to be stupid about something and wasn't going to be talked out of it.

Gage was holding the other end. Of course he was. He'd never once let go of anything in his life.

Otto couldn't climb it. His hands were still a long way off, down in the blue somewhere, learning how to be hands again. He couldn't so much as close them.

He knew where up was, though.

The cool came through, slow. The pull in his arms. He lifted, and set it down. And the rope hung there above him, swaying a little where someone at the top had shifted his grip, and the blue had an up in it, and he lay on the floor of himself and looked at it.
