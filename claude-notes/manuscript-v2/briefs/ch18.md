## The job: two things, and only two
1. **Otto's side of ch. 15's line moving.**
   - The patient voice comes down the shaft: a sentence, then a gap left for a reply.
   - This time **one word almost gets through whole: "Gretchen."** He knows the word. It's the voice's sister, a girl at the gates in Book 1.
   - Because it's a word and not just a rhythm, something in him **reaches.** It's small: a lift, the feeling of a stone shifting in its socket. Then the stillness takes it back.
   - He doesn't know anything moved outside. **We never see the screen.** He only feels the reach and the voice stopping mid-sentence.
   - Then the voice starts the same sentence again and leaves the same gap. That gap is meant for him; he can tell now. He can't fill it. But he knows it's there.
2. **The promise turns from an anchor into a pull.**
   - In ch. 12 the broken "I promise, brother" sat on him like one more stone and held him down.
   - Here, after the reach, he understands it differently. A promise isn't broken until the one who made it stops. He hasn't jumped *yet*.
   - The promise stops weighing down and starts pulling up, **like a rope let down a shaft,** with Gage's face at the top of it, waiting at the hole.
   - **End:** he can't climb it yet. But for the first time the blue has an up.
