## What the chapter must do
1. **The blue, from inside.** Otto's sensory limits:
   - he can't see the room;
   - he feels warm and cool and the pull in his arms, and hears a far-off steady sound;
   - **faint voices reach him without words**, with a rhythm he almost knows (Ben's, sitting with him; ch. 2: "speak to him… he may hear every word").

   He doesn't know whose they are, only that they're *his*.
2. **The stones,** remembered: holding them open over the lava, the nosebleed, the wave, the promise to Gage ("I promise, brother," Book 1). He knows he broke it. *That's what keeps him from coming up:* he made the stillness deep enough to carry three lives, and it closed over him.
3. **A Shu Chan memory, and the seed (new, approved).** Keep it in the shed in Miner Town, before Otto was 8:
   - the blue light at the old man's skin;
   - the breath;
   - "the fist is the last thing";
   - **one night Shu Chan says a word under his breath while he holds a stance: "Kalinga."**

   Otto, six or seven, asked what it meant. Shu Chan said it was the name of the last place he'd stood watch, or he didn't answer, or he said "a door," something partial. Otto always thought it was a prayer, or a place that wasn't there anymore. He still doesn't know.
   - **Don't explain it.** (Later chapters reveal it as the name of an old Ashoka boundary post.)
   - The butterfly may be *remembered* from the shed ("the proof"), but **it must not appear in the blue** in this chapter.
4. **The trap, felt rather than explained.** Every time he tries to rise, the stillness takes the effort and turns it into more calm. "He was not trapped. He was adapted." He matched the room so perfectly there's no edge left to climb out by.
5. **End** on something small and unresolved: the voices going away, the pull coming back, the word *Kalinga* in the blue like a stone he can't lift. **No finger movement yet.**
