## Beats
1. **The asking: Camilla, about 1,300 words.**
   - Flitzberg's rooms, among the dolls. He sends for Camilla, Ben and Gage. Otto is still in bed, weak and awake; one short beat of him on the way.
   - Flitzberg tells them, truthfully under the pact, that **two Grandmasters have asked the table to sit out of season**, which hasn't happened in living memory. One has brought a matter: someone in Ashoka has laid four houses' books side by side and found **the same small leak in all four.** Camilla's mind goes straight to ch6's line. **She knows what the leak is. It's this office.**
   - **The orphan beat (required).** Flitzberg takes out an old assessment file, the examiners' notes from the trial rooms, written in Sef's hand. **Sef** is the grey examiner who annotated recruits for thirty years.
     - He reads one line aloud: **"the girl thinks in exits, the gentle one thinks in patterns, and they listen to each other without ranking."**
     - Optionally the CONTROL line too: "the strong one spent his strength making the others stronger."
     - Optionally his own verdict that night: **"Unlikely heroes. Possible."**
     - **Don't call Ben "the small one"**, because in Book 1 that's Otto.
   - Flitzberg will answer the copyist before the table sits, by sending someone who reads patterns to work beside her **without telling her what he is.** He means Ben.
   - **The conflict (required):** Ben is the mission's shift-counter, and there are five nights left. Gage objects in few words, with his hands open. Ben wants to go. He's afraid of the city above and of himself, and wants it anyway. **Camilla calls it.** He goes, under the ring's cover, and is **back by the third night** for the last rehearsal.
   - Flitzberg's terms: never name the ring, never lie to them unless lying keeps them alive (he says this plainly), and come home.
2. **Topside: Ben, about 1,900 words.** This is the first contact.
   - **Ben's first time in Trinity:** light, height, clean air, terminals on every wrist. He wears one the ring has given him, set to say he's no one in particular: a junior runner for Drona's lower stacks.
     - **He thinks of Gretchen at the gates, below.** The guilt and the wonder.
     - **His father's compass**, lost to the lava in Book 1. His hand goes to his hip, to the pocket that isn't there, a habit since ch9.
   - **The dead room:** Rohan meets him at the door. Rohan's rule broke in ch23. **One look between them:** a commander and a miner boy, each knowing the other is more than he says.
   - **Nayan:** clipped, on the floor among her columns. She tests him: "Read me Chanakya, third year, autumn." He reads it, then reads *across*, and finds something in the forty-day line she hadn't seen, a rhythm. **He doesn't say what it is, because he knows.** That's the dramatic irony: Ben knows this leak is home.
     - **Ben's choice (required):** he protects the ring without lying. He says "I don't know what it is" only if that's true. **Make it true in a narrow sense,** for example he doesn't know *how* it works.
     - **Or he doesn't speak,** and Nayan notices the silence.
     - **Don't break the pact.** It's his strain, not a lie.
   - **Kaan:** the unmarked-door face. Ben, who counts faces, **sees him.** He is the first person who ever has, and Kaan registers it. Kaan writes the file number on paper and lays it down.
   - **The paper road, both sides:**
     - Nayan pulls her copy of the archive's public custody ledger: *one folio, released for one night, author's custody, Ravi*, beneath **the same number.**
     - Two papers, one number, both citable. Ben sees the pattern first: the custody line's date is the same morning Kaan's appointment was set. "He went and got it the morning you asked him to bring it."
     - **The file's meaning stays sealed.** The number is in focus; what it's a number *for* isn't.
   - **Rohan:** "That's the other road." **Not the wall.** He can't say what wall, and he doesn't.
   - **End:** Ben, alone for a moment at a high window, looks **down**. For the first time he can see the Junkyard shelf from above, and somewhere below it, the ring, home. Or end on him writing one dated line in Nayan's style and leaving it in her pile, a gift between pattern-readers.
