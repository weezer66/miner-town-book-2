# Chapter 25: The Door Beneath

*"the Engine is blind without the Eyes. Not as a saying. As fact."*

— Book 1, Chapter 22: The Engine and Eyes

---

Otto's hand opened.

Not a finger this time. The whole hand, slowly, as if Otto were uncurling it around something too heavy to let go.

In the blue, he felt the palm return before he felt the rest of himself. The thumb moved. The fingers followed, one by one, each movement a small refusal of the stillness he had made. His hand opened and closed once. Then it opened again and stayed open.

Master Chan had told him to find one thing that was his. Otto had found the finger. Now he had to trust it with the rest.

He tried the wrist. It turned a fraction, then stopped. He waited through the pull and the cool, through the deep quiet that had once been a place he could enter and leave. The next time the wrist moved, it moved farther. His forearm answered. His elbow bent.

The blue did not break. It loosened.

He could feel the difference between wanting to move and being carried. The first was his. The second had been the room's. He had spent so long making himself into a still thing that he had forgotten the first one could be enough.

He drew his arm toward himself.

The motion dragged through him like a rope pulled up from deep water. His shoulder followed. The muscles in his back woke in pieces. He could not feel the tank around him, not exactly, but he felt the gel shift and the pull of the line at his face. His body was there. It was not the room.

The old man's voice came from farther away now. "Don't try to wake. Find what is yours."

Otto found the edge of his own hand. He found the arm attached to it. He found the weight of his chest, the lift and fall the tank had been making for him. His next breath did not come from the room. It came from him.

He opened his eyes.

Light blurred above him. A face leaned into view, then moved back, giving him space. The medic did not ask him to speak. She watched his eyes settle, checked the line at his arm, and keyed the call.

The alert went out without a name. Three rooms away, three people who had asked to be woken for anything sat up at once.

The medic did not open the tank. The condition had been plain: the three of them had to be there, and all three had to say so. She held the system in its safe cycle while the gel kept Otto's body supported. When Gage, Ben, and Camilla reached the glass, they saw his eyes open and his hand resting against the inside of the tank.

Gage put both palms to the glass. Ben stood at his shoulder. Camilla took one step closer, then stopped, as if she were afraid the sight of her would pull Otto back under.

The medic asked them, one at a time. Gage said yes. Ben said yes. Camilla said yes last.

The release took time. The gel drained by stages, the breathing line came free, and two medics supported Otto's shoulders as they moved him to the bed beside the tank. By the time the others came around the partition, he was sitting upright, a blanket across his lap, his eyes open but still trying to make a world from the light.

"Otto," Gage said.

Otto looked at him. His lips moved once before sound came. "How long?"

"Long enough," Ben said, then stopped. He had no better answer.

Camilla leaned over the bed so Otto did not have to lift his head to see her. "The trial ended. We stayed. You didn't wake, so they kept you in the tank. We made them promise not to open it unless we were all here." She paused. "You are out now. That's what you need first."

Otto's gaze moved to her metal arm. He looked at it, then at her face.

"I know," she said. "I'll tell you the rest when your head is ready for it."

He lowered his eyes to his right hand. It shook once against the blanket. He closed it, opened it again, and held it where he could see it.

---

The fiber archive opened at first bell.

Ravi entered through the public gate with his terminal on. He needed the gate to know who he was. Beyond it, he left the terminal dark in his pocket and used the paper index, following the old number from one bound register to another.

The number led to a sealed case. The findings were above his grade. He knew that. He was not asking for the findings.

At the retrieval desk, he wrote the request by hand: the paper original of the case, for verification against the fiber record. The clerk looked at the number, then at him.

"This case is sealed above your grade."

"I know. I filed the report. I want the original folio, not a terminal rendering."

She checked the register. The disposition was sealed above his grade; the source folio could be inspected by its originating analyst in person. She brought it in its fiber sleeve and recorded the handoff in the paper custody ledger. Ravi signed his name beneath the file number.

The paper was real. It had no terminal copy to replace it, no screen that could be changed while he looked away. The old ink was his. The date was the one he remembered. He read the folio once, slowly, and found the part the records had never explained to him. He did not let his face change.

He returned the folio to its sleeve and carried it out. The custody ledger would show that he had taken it. That was a trail he could choose to leave. A terminal trail was not.

The clerk asked when he would return it.

"Tomorrow," Ravi said.

It was not a promise he knew he could keep. He slid the fiber sleeve inside his coat, beside the two slips, and walked out through the public gate with his terminal still dark in his pocket.

Elara woke before the terminal sounded.

In the dream, the man had stopped at the door and turned toward her. The light was poor, but she knew the set of his mouth, the hard attention in his eyes. She had seen him across the monitoring floor, in the white afterimage of the boys' feeds. She had heard him accuse her: You made the system lie for you.

Daren.

She sat up. For a few seconds she did not know whether she had remembered him or dreamed him into the half-seen face. The corridor had not given her enough to swear to. The dream was not evidence. But the face in it had come from somewhere real.

She pulled on her clothes and started for Rohan's quarters. She would tell him what she thought, and what she could not prove. Before she reached the stair, her terminal gave one short pulse. A sealed summons from the Ancient One: beneath the archive, now.

The Ancient One was waiting in the chamber with the grey wall. The columns had gone dark. A paper map lay open on the table between them. Rohan was not there.

"You saw the hand return," the Ancient One said.

"I saw a man. I think it was Daren."

"What made you think that?"

Elara told her about the face in the dream, then about the monitoring floor after the twenty-four-hour shift: Daren's anger, his accusation, the way he had stood close enough for her to see him clearly. She kept the two things separate. "The corridor gave me a shape. The dream gave me the name. My memory may be joining them because I expect it to."

The Ancient One watched her for a moment. "Good. Do not turn recognition into proof."

"I won't. I was going to tell Rohan."

"You may tell him what you saw and what you suspect. But do not contact the Master of the Reserve. Not directly. If he learns that we have named him before we know what he did, the next move may be his, and the tide may turn before we can measure it."

Elara looked at the map. Four houses marked the upper cities. Below them, where the public maps left a blank at the old Interchange, the paper showed a ring with no crest.

"What is that?"

"The place the maps leave out." The Ancient One placed one finger on the ring. "After the six days of fighting, the four Grandmasters understood that none of their houses could be trusted to hold the balance alone. So they made an institution that belonged to none of them, with authority to reach all four. They called it Patal Loka."

Elara looked from the map to the blank wall around it. "A fifth house?"

"No. A house has a flag, a public face, and a claim to its own power. Patal Loka has none. It works beneath the boundary, in the old ring, where no gana can say it was built on its ground. Its people come from all four houses. Its work is to keep one house from becoming powerful enough to rule the rest. Quietly. Before a correction becomes a war."

"Who knows it exists?"

"The four sitting Grandmasters, and the people who serve it. Each Grandmaster is told once, at succession. The retired ones keep silent. Until now, no one outside that circle has been trusted with its name."

Elara understood the cost of what she had just been given. It was not only information. It was a door that could not be closed again.

"Why tell me?"

"Because you have seen the archive and still asked how to prove what it shows. You brought me a suspicion and named the parts you could not prove. That is fortitude, and it is loyalty to the truth before it is loyalty to a house. I need more minds in this now."

Elara looked again at the ring on the paper. "Then let me work. Not under a title. Not in a roster. I can trace the public records around the hand's path, find what can be proved without the Sākṣī. I can help from the shadows."

"You will not use the hidden archive as evidence. You will not make an accusation from a face in bad light. And you will not act in Ashoka's name."

"I understand."

"Rohan decides what he tells the Dhammapala. That choice belongs to him."

Elara nodded. She could tell Rohan about Daren. She could not tell him the name on the map unless the Ancient One chose to extend the trust. For now, that boundary would hold.

"I will call the Master of the Reserve," the Ancient One said. "You will listen. You will not speak."

She crossed to the old call plate set into the stone at the far end of the chamber. It had no screen and no house insignia, only four shallow marks around a blank center. She turned the mark for Kubera. The chamber stayed dark.

A voice answered from the stone. "Ancient One."

"The Guard is active. It was seen in Drona last night."

Silence held for a breath.

"You have proof?"

"Not proof I can put before a council. I have a witness, and a pattern."

"Who is the witness?"

"Someone I trust. That is all you need tonight."

Another pause. Elara heard the faint scrape of something being set down at the other end: paper, perhaps, or one of the rings the Master wore when he spoke for Kubera.

"Where do you want to meet?"

"At the ring. Patal Loka. Before you make your next move."

The answer came quickly. "I will come without escort."

The Ancient One turned the Kubera mark back to its place. The line went silent.

Elara looked at the paper map. "And I stay out of the room."

"You stay where you can see the work and where no one can see you doing it," said the Ancient One. "That is what the shadows are for."

For the first time since the corridor, Elara felt the shape of the next step. Not a charge. Not an accusation. A meeting beneath the boundary, with a man who had agreed to come before he knew what she knew.

Rohan was waiting in the dead room when she returned. He read her face before she spoke.

"I think the hand was Daren," she said. "I saw him in the monitor room. The face in the corridor was too dim to prove it, and the dream is only memory rearranging itself."

Rohan looked at the paper map between them. "The Ancient One believes you?"

"She believes I saw something. She has called the Master of the Reserve to meet her."

"And the Dhammapala?"

"That is your decision."

He looked down at the terminal on the table. He did not reach for it. Elara could not tell whether he was weighing the risk of telling his Grandmaster or the cost of keeping the warning to himself. She left the choice with him.
