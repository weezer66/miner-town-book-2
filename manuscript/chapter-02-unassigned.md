# Chapter 2: Unassigned

The room didn't answer. It simply held the implication in place: if the monitoring team was already surprised, then whatever was happening in Otto's intake was not a routine anomaly. It was the kind of reading that made people rethink the system itself.

Rohan leaned forward, the room's usual boredom evaporating in an instant. "That kind of spike? That's not a diet issue. That's a system fault."

Elara's eyes stayed on the screen. "He wasn't just built differently. The scanner is reading him almost like an energy source."

The silence that followed wasn't casual. It was the silence of recognition. Trinity's baseline readings were designed to be stable, predictable, and easy to classify. An energy surge that high on the initiation monitor wasn't a normal physiological deviation. It was a signal. And signals like that didn't just appear in the middle of a routine intake.

Rohan leaned forward again, his expression sharpening. "That's not a reading error. That's a signature."

Elara's jaw tightened. "Then who does that?"

The question hung in the room, and for a moment, none of them moved. In a system built to convert bodies into metrics and pain into protocol, a reading that looked like a source rather than a subject was the kind of thing that made people wonder whether the machine was tracking the wrong thing entirely.

It was the first true crack in the room's composure.

"He isn't just different," Rowan said quietly. "He's outside the system's vocabulary."

Daren gave a small, contemptuous snort. "And yet here we are. Still pretending the categories matter."

No one answered him. Not because he was wrong, but because for the first time since the queue had gone red, the four of them had the same realization at once: Trinity had never been built to account for this.

And that, more than the reading itself, was what frightened them.
